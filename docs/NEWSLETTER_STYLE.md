# Logan's newsletter design and voice

Owner's standing direction, October 29, 2026. Apply to future website newsletters and Brevo email drafts. Read alongside [NEWSLETTERS.md](NEWSLETTERS.md).

## A personal letter

Write like Logan talking with a supporter across a table: conversational, honest, clear and warm. Clean up spelling without turning his voice into formal ministry language. Explain why an experience mattered, rather than just listing activities. Avoid exaggerated spiritual language, invented conversations, ministry results or emotions. Use Scripture only when Logan supplies or approves a passage that fits his reflection. His own experiences and words are the source; headings and footage are not evidence of an unwritten story.

## Existing website is the design reference

Keep the site's identity, header/navigation, spacing and colors. Reuse `public/styles.css` tokens: forest green, deep green, cream, restrained coral, Playfair Display headings and DM Sans body text. Use readable text columns, generous breathing room, simple headings and subtle accents. Avoid bulletin styling, ornamental religious graphics, report-like panels and a donation-led layout. Email uses reliable Georgia heading / Arial body fallbacks with the same palette.

October 30 refinement: the newsletter should be similar to the website without duplicating its page layout. Use a cream paper sheet, a compact “Letters from Logan” masthead and smaller headings. The website opening may put the title alongside the cover on desktop and stack on mobile. Keep portrait photo pairs compact, including on phones, so they do not overwhelm the letter. Email uses the same paper treatment in a simpler single column.

## Natural flow

Start with a personal hello and where life is at. Move through life and ministry stories, what God is teaching Logan, what is ahead, specific prayer requests, and a personal thank-you. Headings may change or combine; these are a flow, not a mandatory checklist of reports. Keep support as an optional quiet text link near the end, after the personal closing/signature. Never imply that funds are the reader's main role.

## Real photography

Use supplied personal photographs and authentic frames from supplied videos. Keep originals unchanged and outside the public package. Preserve photo proportions; optimize delivery copies without stretching or unnecessary crops. A large opening photo, occasional full-width photographs and a restrained two-column gallery are sufficient. Place each photo beside its related story, with short factual captions when helpful. Do not substitute stock or AI imagery. Do not identify people, describe service roles or name occasions from guesses. Video stills should be identified as such in captions or the media notes. Keep actual videos outside email attachments; agree on hosting and use a linked thumbnail when needed.

Latest photo treatment preference (replaces the earlier feathered-edge request): keep the complete photograph with crisp square edges and no fade or crop. Put a muted pale green backing layer behind it to form a small mat/border, with a subtle offset backing on the website. Email uses a simple compatible table mat. Preserve the original image and proportions. Confirmed first-letter captions: “Diakonos class, discussing the Trinity.” The other table photo shows another DTS learning, growing and building community; the owner wants its caption to reflect the communal, relational nature of life there.

## Website and email

Use the same approved writing, image choices and story order from the shared content file. The website may have more visual breathing room; the email remains a simple mobile-friendly letter with compatible inline styles. Include a clear website/share link, subscribe option and Brevo-managed unsubscribe. Keep prayer and thanks more prominent than giving.

## Review

Keep unfinished writing visibly labeled in private previews. Do not invent missing Kona stories, coffee ministry details, plans or prayer requests. Preview desktop, narrow mobile and email layouts. Publishing and sending require the existing owner approvals; approval to design does not send an email.
